## Escalation levels
Level 1: the operator handles the alert within the standing procedures. Level 2: the duty engineer is consulted for confirmed alerts affecting safety or supply, or any alert requiring a maintenance commitment. Level 3: the fleet or asset manager is involved for repeated alerts on one asset and decisions with major cost or availability impact.

Each level owns a documented time budget for responding.

## Escalation criteria
Escalate when the interval is confirmed beyond the threshold and the required action is irreversible or costly; when several assets show correlated alerts suggesting a common cause; when triage load prevents timely handling; or when a needed confirmation run cannot be scheduled inside the risk window. Escalation is cheap; a missed escalation window is not.

When in doubt, escalate early rather than late; a level-2 call that resolves nothing still costs less than a missed window.

## Documentation
Every escalation records the alert interval bounds, the coverage level, the knowledge sources consulted and the decision taken. Advice that cannot be traced to a source does not by itself justify an escalation decision, and the written record is what makes later audits of alert and escalation quality possible.

Keep the record short and factual; it is written for the next reviewer, not for the archive alone.
