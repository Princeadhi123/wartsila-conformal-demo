## 2025-03-14 - Vessel A: offline wash restored kMc
kMc estimated 0.968 at 15 kn with the whole interval below the 0.975 action threshold and fuel flow 2.1 percent above baseline. An offline detergent wash during the port call restored kMc to 0.991 at the matched load point, and fuel flow fell 2.1 percent at 15 kn. The wash was judged worthwhile and the assessment interval was shortened for the summer fouling season.

## 2025-06-02 - Vessel B: online wash, partial recovery
kMc 0.979 at 18 kn, watch state with the interval straddling the threshold. An online wash during the loaded passage recovered kMc to 0.986: a partial recovery consistent with mature deposits rather than loose surface fouling. An offline crank wash was scheduled for the next docking rather than repeating online washes that were giving diminishing returns.

The entry closed with a recommendation to prefer offline washing once deposits harden.

## 2025-08-19 - Vessel C: alert traced to inlet filter
A kMc alert at low speed with depressed P2 led to an inlet inspection that found a salt-crusted filter stage and a bypass leak at one seal. After the refit, kMc re-estimated at 0.993: the apparent decay had been inlet depression, not compressor fouling. Sensor validation and a filter check were added to the alert playbook as mandatory first steps.

## 2025-10-30 - Vessel A: wash deferred, confirmed later
kMc 0.971 watch at 9 kn with a wide interval at low load. A confirmation run at 20 kn produced kMc 0.966, fully below threshold, so the alert was confirmed and the wash performed at the next port call with recovery to 0.990. The watch-plus-confirmation sequence avoided an unnecessary early wash and is now the standard handling for low-load alerts.
