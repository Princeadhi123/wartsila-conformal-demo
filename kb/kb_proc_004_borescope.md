## Prerequisites
Engine stopped, cooled below the access temperature limit and isolated; borescope access ports opened; the previous inspection photo set available for side-by-side comparison. Allow enough cooling time; inspecting a hot engine damages the borescope and distorts the findings. Have the fleet's findings-classification atlas and the alert history on hand.

Confirm turning-gear operation and that gas-path temperatures have equalised enough for safe access.

## Inspection steps
1. Motor the shaft slowly on turning gear and inspect each accessible blade row systematically.
2. Photograph leading-edge erosion, coating loss, discoloration typical of hot corrosion, tip rubbing and any foreign-object damage.
3. Classify each finding by extent as none, light, moderate or severe using the fleet's standard photo atlas.
4. Give special attention to the first-stage nozzle guide vanes and the first blade row, which see the hottest gas.
5. Record tip-clearance indications where the tooling allows.

## Findings and action
Light erosion with a stable trend: continue monitoring at the normal interval. Moderate findings: shorten the inspection interval and review the operating profile and fuel quality. Severe findings, or rapid progression between inspections: de-rate the engine or plan repair, and do not wait for the next scheduled opening. File the photo set and classification against the alert that triggered the inspection.
