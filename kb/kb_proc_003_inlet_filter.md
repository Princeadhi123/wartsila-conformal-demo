## When to inspect
Inspect the inlet filter when fouling-related alerts fire, after operations in dusty or salt-laden air, when filter differential pressure is unusually high, or on the planned interval. A clogged or leaking filter raises inlet depression and can masquerade as compressor decay, so this check is cheap insurance before committing to a wash.

Include it as a mandatory early step in any confirmed kMc alert's checklist before wash work is scheduled.

## Inspection steps
1. Record filter differential pressure before stopping the airflow.
2. Isolate the intake and open the filter house; photograph the stage condition.
3. Check for salt crust, oil film, dust loading and bypass leakage around seals.
4. Check drain scuppers for blockage.
5. Replace or clean elements within the maker's limits; oil-soaked or damaged media must be replaced, not washed.

## Acceptance criteria
Differential pressure within the maker's limits after refit, no visible bypass paths, and findings logged with date, vessel and operating hours. If the filter was the cause, the apparent kMc decay should largely disappear after refit; re-estimate at a steady load point before scheduling any wash, and keep the photographs for the maintenance record.

A rising trend in differential pressure over weeks is itself a fouling-season indicator.
