## Fouling mechanisms
Airborne salt, dust, oil vapour and industrial particulates deposit on compressor blades and vanes. Deposits roughen blade surfaces, reduce the effective flow area and lower compressor efficiency and pressure ratio. Fouling is gradual and partly recoverable by washing; erosion and blade damage are not. The fouling rate depends on ambient air quality, humidity and operating hours since the last wash, and it accelerates in warm, salt-laden air.

## Observable symptoms
At constant gas-generator speed and ambient conditions, fouling shows as reduced compressor outlet pressure P2, increased compressor outlet temperature T2 (lower isentropic efficiency), higher fuel flow for the same delivered torque, and reduced achievable shaft speed at full lever. The decay coefficient kMc estimated from these measurements falls below 1.0; a drop of roughly 0.02-0.03 typically corresponds to a fuel penalty on the order of 1-3 percent.

## Distinguishing recoverable decay
Compare trends before and after washing. Fouling recovers most of the lost kMc after a proper wash; erosion, foreign-object damage or sensor drift do not. If P2 recovers but T2 stays high, suspect instrumentation rather than the gas path. Track the rate of kMc decline: a step change suggests damage or a sensor fault, while a smooth multi-week decline is consistent with fouling.
