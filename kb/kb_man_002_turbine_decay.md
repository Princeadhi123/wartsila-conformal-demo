## Degradation mechanisms
HP turbine decay, seen as a falling kMt coefficient, arises from blade erosion by ingested particles, hot corrosion when fuel or sea-salt contaminants attack blade coatings at metal temperature, and increased tip clearance from casing distortion or blade rubs. Unlike compressor fouling these mechanisms are largely non-recoverable without overhaul, so the response is inspect, limit and plan rather than wash.

## Observable symptoms
Rising HP turbine exit temperature T48 at constant power is the primary symptom: a less efficient turbine passes hotter gas downstream. The exit pressure P48 may drift, gas-generator speed for a given power tends to rise, and fuel flow increases for the same delivered power. Because symptoms overlap with compressor fouling, always consider both coefficients together rather than treating a low kMt estimate in isolation.

## Distinguishing real decay from sensor faults
A step change in T48, or one probe drifting away from the pack, indicates a sensor fault rather than blade decay; validate instrumentation first. A smooth multi-week T48 rise with stable P48 spread and rising fuel flow is consistent with genuine degradation. Confirm with a borescope inspection before committing to de-rating or repair, since both carry real cost and availability impact.
