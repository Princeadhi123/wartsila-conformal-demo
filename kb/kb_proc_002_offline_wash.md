## Prerequisites
Engine stopped, cooled below the manual's wash temperature limit, and isolated; turning gear available; drain paths open and wash-fluid containment in place. An offline crank wash soaks and agitates deposits mechanically and restores most of the recoverable kMc, at the cost of several hours of unavailability. Plan it for a port call or a scheduled layover, and confirm demineralised water and detergent stocks before starting.

## Procedure steps
1. Shut down and allow the gas generator to cool below the manual's wash temperature limit.
2. Isolate fuel and ignition, then engage the turning gear and motor the shaft at cranking speed.
3. Apply a pre-soak of demineralised water for the prescribed time.
4. Inject detergent solution in stages while cranking and observe drain fluid for solids.
5. Rinse with demineralised water until the drain water runs clear and pH-neutral.
6. Dry-crank the shaft, drain all sumps and remove the isolations.
7. Perform a short test run to idle, check for abnormal vibration or exhaust temperature spread, then release to service.

## Acceptance criteria
Target kMc recovery to within about 0.005 of the post-overhaul baseline at a matched load point, and verify fuel flow at the reference speed is reduced relative to pre-wash. Log pre- and post-wash kMc, fluid volumes and any solids found in the drains. If recovery falls well short of target, suspect non-recoverable damage and plan a borescope inspection.

Record ambient conditions at the matched point for a fair comparison.
